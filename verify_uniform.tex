{\Large\bfseries Uniform failure of the canonical product form\\[3pt]
in limited pass-and-swap queues}\par
\textit{Working research proof and reproducible finite checks}\hfill September 6, 2026

\begin{abstract}
For every finite swap limit $w\ge2$, we give identical, strictly positive service allocations on a closed tandem with swapping graph $C_{2w+1}$ for which the canonical OI product form fails on a single recurrent class. The class consists of balanced orientations with branch lengths $w,w+1$. We prove its irreducibility under positive service at every position. With one class at rate two and every other class at rate one, the unnormalized stationary-balance defect at an explicit state is $-w/(2w+2)!$. More generally, every nonunit rate of the distinguished class gives a nonzero defect. The calculation compares limited and unlimited dynamics: exactly two incoming head-completion flows exchange destinations. The proof uses no stationary-distribution solver or extrapolation from finite tests.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and uniform statement}
Use the closed tandem and explicit first-later-compatible replacement rule of Dorsman--Gardner~\cite{DG} and Comte--Dorsman~\cite{CD}. A state $s=(c,d)$ contains one job of each label $0,\ldots,2w$, in head-to-tail queue order. Cycle neighbors are compatible. A completing job replaces the first later compatible job, whose occupant repeats the rule strictly farther back. Stop after $w$ replacements, or when no later neighbor exists. The current job then joins the other queue's tail. An event's rate is the \emph{initiating} job's service rate.

Orient each edge forward in $L(c,d)=c\Vert\rev(d)$. Let $\B$ consist of states whose orientation is the union of two directed paths, with disjoint interiors, from one source to one sink, of lengths $w$ and $w+1$. In both queues give every job its class rate,
\[
 r_0=\theta>0,\qquad r_i=1\ (1\le i\le2w),\qquad
 \mu(q)=\nu(q)=\sum_{x\in q}r_x.
\]
This is an OI allocation: the total rate is permutation-invariant, and every prefix increment is the positive rate of the appended job. Define the canonical weights
\[
 \F(q)=\prod_{j=1}^{|q|}\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{j}r_{q_i}},\qquad
 \F(\varnothing)=1,\qquad \W(c,d)=\F(c)\F(d).
\]
These are the weights in~\cite[Theorem 3 and Eq.\ (25)]{DG}. Generators act on row vectors.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $w\ge2$ and $\theta>0$, $\B$ is a single closed communicating class. At
\[
 d_w=(w,w-1,\ldots,1,w+1,w+2,\ldots,2w),\qquad
 s_w=((0),d_w),
\]
the limited generator restricted to $\B$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 (\W\Qw)(s_w)=
 \frac{1}{w!\prod_{k=w}^{2w}(\theta+k)}
 -\frac{1}{(2w)!(\theta+2w)}.
\end{equation}
This is nonzero for every $\theta\ne1$. Consequently the unique stationary law on $\B$ is not proportional to $\W$. At $\theta=2$ the defect is exactly $-w/(2w+2)!$.
\end{theorem}

\section{The balanced region is a recurrent class}
\begin{lemma}[Closure]\label{lem:closure}
$\B$ is nonempty and closed under every position completion, irrespective of the nonnegative service rates assigned to those completions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Compare a completion in $c$ with its unlimited version. Unlimited P\&S preserves the placement orientation~\cite[Lemma 1, p.\ 217]{DG}. Its replacement chain follows a directed path, so has length at most $w+1$. If its length is at most $w$, the limited and unlimited transitions agree.

Otherwise it traverses the full longer branch from the unique source $u$ to the unique sink $v$. Both endpoints are in $c$. Every vertex lies between them in $L$, so all jobs are in $c$, with $u$ its head and $v$ its tail. Let $p$ precede $v$ on that branch. The limited completion ejects $p$; the unlimited completion makes one more replacement and ejects $v$. For the same remaining word $R$, their resulting placement orders are $Rvp$ and $Rpv$. Only the edge $p\to v$ reverses. The longer branch loses one edge, and the shorter branch gains the reversed edge: the lengths remain $w,w+1$. Queue exchange reverses orientations, proving the same assertion for $d$.

There are $2(2w+1)$ balanced orientations. Each has $\binom{2w-1}{w}$ linear extensions, obtained by interleaving the two internal chains, and each extension has $2w+2$ queue cuts. Thus
\[
 |\B|=2(2w+1)(2w+2)\binom{2w-1}{w}>0.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Irreducibility with positive position rates]\label{lem:irreducible}
If every position of every nonempty queue has a positive completion rate, then $\B$ is one communicating class.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First fix one orientation. A tail completion transfers a job to the other tail and moves the cut in $L$ by one without changing $L$. Both directions are possible. Adjacent incomparable labels $a,b$ in a linear extension can also be exchanged without a swap: put the cut after the pair, so the state is $(Pab,\rev(T))$. Complete $a$. Since $a,b$ are not adjacent in the swapping graph, $a$ departs directly, producing $(Pb,\rev(T)a)$ and the new linear extension $PbaT$. Tail moves restore any desired cut. The reverse exchange is possible in the same way. Any two linear extensions are linked by adjacent incomparable exchanges: successively move the next desired label left, across labels incomparable to it. Therefore all states with the fixed orientation communicate using only zero-replacement completions.

Index balanced orientations by $(u,\varepsilon)$, where $u$ is the source and $\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}$ is the direction of the longer branch, with arithmetic modulo $n=2w+1$. Put all jobs in $c$, with the longer branch's internal vertices before the shorter branch's. A head completion follows the longer branch and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:closure}, reverses its last edge. The new orientation is $(u,-\varepsilon)$. Applying the same argument in $d$ to the reversed orientation moves the source to its neighbor on the old longer branch, giving $(u+\varepsilon,-\varepsilon)$. Hence both moves
\[
 (u,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow(u,-\varepsilon),\qquad
 (u,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow(u+\varepsilon,-\varepsilon)
\]
are realizable. Each is an involution; their composition moves $u$ by $\varepsilon$ while retaining that direction. They connect all $2n$ orientations. The within-orientation communication just proved lets these moves be concatenated from arbitrary states. Thus $\B$ is strongly connected.
\end{proof}

All our class rates are positive, so Lemmas~\ref{lem:closure}--\ref{lem:irreducible} apply. In particular every state of $\B$ is recurrent. Since $C_{2w+1}$ is three-colorable, its color classes can be refined to $w+1$ nonempty independent sets. The recurrent height-$w+1$ region therefore also contradicts Conjecture~1 of~\cite{DG} for each $w\ge2$.

\section{Only two incoming flows change}
Let $\Qi$ be the unlimited generator on $\B$. Each fixed-orientation set is closed under unlimited P\&S and is irreducible by the zero-replacement argument above. The unlimited product-form theorem~\cite[Theorem 3]{DG} gives $\W\Qi=0$ on each such set, hence on their union $\B$. No common normalization across orientations is needed for this unnormalized balance identity.

Write
\begin{align*}
 x_w&=(w,w-1,\ldots,1,0,w+1,w+2,\ldots,2w),\\
 y_w&=(w,w+1,w-1,\ldots,1,w+2,\ldots,2w,0),\\
 X_w&=(\varnothing,x_w),\qquad Y_w=(\varnothing,y_w),\\
 s'_w&=((2w),(w,w-1,\ldots,1,w+1,\ldots,2w-1,0)).
\end{align*}
All four states $X_w,Y_w,s_w,s'_w$ are balanced. The head completions at $X_w,Y_w$ both have rate one, since their initiating label is $w\ne0$. They exchange destinations:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &\text{limit }w&\text{unlimited}\\ \hline
 X_w\text{, head of }d&s_w&s'_w\\
 Y_w\text{, head of }d&s'_w&s_w
\end{array}
\]
Indeed, their unrestricted replacement chains are respectively
\[
 w,w-1,\ldots,1,0,2w\qquad\text{and}\qquad
 w,w+1,w+2,\ldots,2w,0.
\]
Both contain $w+1$ replacements. Cutting off their last replacement produces the displayed limited destinations.

\begin{lemma}[Completeness of the two-flow calculation]\label{lem:two}
Among events with different limited and unlimited destinations, the only gain of incoming flow to $s_w$ is the head completion at $X_w$, and the only loss is the head completion at $Y_w$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:closure}, any changed event from $\B$ is a head completion with all jobs in that queue and an unlimited chain of length $w+1$. To enter $s_w$, the completing queue must be $d$, previously containing all jobs.

For a limited arrival at $s_w$, the ejected penultimate vertex is $0$, while the unchanged final vertex of the old long branch is the last entry of $d_w$, namely $2w$. The cycle has only one directed simple path of length $w+1$ with final edge $0\to2w$: its labels are $w,w-1,\ldots,0,2w$. Reversing the replacements along this fixed path in the output word $d_w$ uniquely recovers $x_w$.

For an unlimited arrival at $s_w$, the final ejected vertex is $0$, and its predecessor is the last entry $2w$ in the output word. The long chain must therefore be $w,w+1,\ldots,2w,0$. Reversing those replacements in $d_w$ uniquely recovers $y_w$. To see uniqueness directly, each retained chain label occupies the position previously occupied by the next chain label; replacing these back and prepending the removed head reconstructs the original word. Thus there are no further changed incoming events.
\end{proof}

The generator diagonals agree: imposing a swap limit retargets completion events without changing their rates, and every event changes the queue lengths. All unchanged incoming events cancel. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:retarget}
 (\W\Qw)(s_w)
 =\bigl(\W(\Qw-\Qi)\bigr)(s_w)
 =\W(X_w)-\W(Y_w).
\end{equation}
This is the uniform reduction: \emph{two source weights suffice, independently of $w$.}

\section{The balance calculation and its scope}
In $x_w$, label $0$ is at position $w+1$: the first $w$ jobs have unit rate, followed by prefix totals $\theta+w,\ldots,\theta+2w$. In $y_w$, label $0$ is last. Hence
\[
 \W(X_w)=\frac{1}{w!\prod_{k=w}^{2w}(\theta+k)},\qquad
 \W(Y_w)=\frac{1}{(2w)!(\theta+2w)}.
\]
Together with~\eqref{eq:retarget}, this proves~\eqref{eq:main}.

The sign is explicit. Let $P_w(\theta)=\prod_{k=w}^{2w-1}(\theta+k)$. It is strictly increasing for $\theta>0$, and $P_w(1)=(2w)!/w!$. Therefore
\[
 (\W\Qw)(s_w)=\frac{1}{\theta+2w}
 \left(\frac{1}{w!P_w(\theta)}-\frac{1}{(2w)!}\right)
\]
is positive for $0<\theta<1$ and negative for $\theta>1$. Normalization by
$Z_{w,\theta}=\sum_{s\in\B}\W(s)>0$ cannot remove this nonzero defect. At rate two,
\[
 W_2(X_w)=\frac{w+1}{(2w+2)!},\qquad
 W_2(Y_w)=\frac{2w+1}{(2w+2)!},\qquad
 \boxed{(W_2Q_{w,2})(s_w)=-\frac{w}{(2w+2)!}.}
\]
This completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. For $w=2$, the general identity reduces to the earlier five-job calculation
\[
 (\W Q_{2,\theta})(s_2)=
 -\frac{(\theta-1)(\theta+6)}{24(\theta+2)(\theta+3)(\theta+4)}.
\]

\subsection*{Scope}
This proves failure of the \emph{canonical} product $\F(c)\F(d)$ on a recurrent class for every $w\ge2$, not failure of every possible alternative factorization $A(c)B(d)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:irreducible} requires positive service at every position. With zero non-head rates, this argument proves closure and existence of a recurrent class in $\B$, but not general irreducibility. The short-orientation Theorem~7 of~\cite{DG} is unaffected, and the $w=1$ bipartite case remains unresolved here.
